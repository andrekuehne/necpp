Infinite-array periodic boundary and Floquet port: evaluation

Status

Evaluation with design decisions, 2026-11-18. The findings come from reading the engine and the WASM layer. Formulas are written from the standard literature; items that need verification before implementation are marked [verify]. Cost figures are estimates.

Implemented so far: stage 1, the periodic kernel unit, tested in the native [periodic_green] suite:

  • issue #20: lattice setup with Gauss reduction, the complex error function and the Ewald evaluation of G_p and G_smooth with gradients. The spatial, spectral and Ewald formulas below are verified against direct lattice sums with loss, the spectral series and independence of the splitting parameter.
  • issue #21: the field of a segment under a smooth kernel. Its sign and normalization are verified against efld_compute (see Field of a segment under the smooth kernel).

Stage 2 so far:

  • issue #22: lattice and scan phases on nec_context and the stateful model, the smooth correction in cmww, the C ABI entry points and createNecPeriodicModel() in the WASM package. Free space only; symmetric geometry is refused rather than unfolded. Verified at the package seam against finite arrays driven with uniform phase progression and a Gaussian amplitude taper:

    • broadside scan impedance of a half-wave dipole cell against the centre of a 15 x 15 array, 3.7 % (1.6 % at 21 x 21, so the difference is the finite array's edge error);
    • the sign of the scan phases, with an asymmetric two-dipole cell at psi = (1.2, 0.5): the finite mutual impedance matches Z01(psi) to 5.7 % and misses Z01(-psi) by 100 %. A one-port cell cannot show the sign, because reciprocity makes its Z(psi) = Z(-psi).

    Also verified: the approach to the isolated element at large spacing, reciprocity Z(psi) = Z(-psi)^T to 2.5e-4, and power closure (Re Z equals the (0,0) Floquet-mode power to 1e-3 at 21 segments, checked natively). A uniformly driven finite array is no usable reference: its centre converges only like N^(-1/2) and is off by tens of percent at 11 x 11.

  • issue #23: any lattice and multi-element unit cells. Lattice presets (square, rectangular, centered rectangular, hexagonal with rows along x or y) and the scan direction (theta, phi) live in TypeScript only; the engine keeps taking two vectors. setLattice refuses a lattice under which a conductor touches or nearly touches a periodic image: the axis distance of a segment to any image segment (its own or another conductor's) is at most the two radii plus NEC's joining tolerance SMIN times the longer segment. Verified at the package seam against tapered finite arrays at oblique scan: rectangular 4.1 % (9 cells, sin theta = 0.61), centered rectangular 2.8 % (sin theta = 0.41), hexagonal 2.3 % (sin theta = 0.58), and a honeycomb cell (hexagonal lattice, two dipoles) 3.7 % on all four entries of its non-reciprocal 2 x 2 scan impedance; broadside differs from the same finite results by 19 to 29 %. Oblique lattice vectors and their reduced equivalent agree to 8e-15 for the same physical scan state, and a scan direction gives the same result as its scan phases.
  • issue #24: close images. For each observation point (segment centre) and source segment, the images of the source within max(2 L, 40 a) of the observation point (L, a the source segment's length and radius; capped at rkh / 2, below efld_compute's lumped approximation) are taken out of G_smooth and evaluated through efld_compute with a displaced source and the scan phase factor. Images are chosen by distance per pair, not as a fixed 3x3 ring, so skewed lattices and cells larger than one lattice step need nothing special, and distant pairs keep the exact Gauss-integrated route. Measured with collinear half-wave dipoles (11 segments, radius 0.001 wavelengths) against a reference that sends every image within 40 segment lengths through efld_compute (lumped approximation off): the filament route was off by 7.6e-5 at a 0.02 wavelength end gap and 3.6e-4 at 0.003; the thin-wire route stays at 5.7e-6 for any reach of one segment length or more. The error was never the Gauss rule (6 and 64 points agree to 3e-7 at a 0.003 gap) but the filament model of a close image, whose difference from the thin-wire kernel grows like (a/d)^2; hence the reach in radii (at 5 mm radius the jump where an image switches route is 9e-5 with two segment lengths, 3.3e-6 with 40 radii). Verified at the package seam: a stack of parallel dipoles 10 wire radii apart gives the same scan impedance as a one-dipole cell and as a two-dipole supercell to 3.0e-6 (9.5e-4 before); the jump where an image switches route is 2.1e-6 (1 mm wires) and 3.3e-6 (5 mm); cells without close images are unchanged. Collinear dipoles with 5 mm gaps match a tapered finite array to 1.2 %, which is the finite array's edge error and larger than the close-image effect, so that case guards only against gross errors.
  • issue #25: accuracy reporting and refusals. One fixed tolerance, 1e-5 relative to the largest scan-impedance entry (nec_periodic_accuracy.h), and no numerical parameters in the API. Every result carries an estimated error and a point status. The estimate covers the solver's numerics: the Ewald kernel (1e-10), the 6-point Gauss rule over the longest segment (1e-10 max(1, (L / 0.1)^10), L in wavelengths, at least four times the error measured on lattices of 0.5, 1 and 3 wavelengths) and the rounding near the Floquet mode nearest to cut-off (eps / (|kz| / k)). Refused instead of loosened: cells whose spectral sum would need more than 10000 modes per evaluation (about 320 square wavelengths; the cost grows with the cell area, 22 s for one 11-segment dipole in a 40 wavelength cell), segments above about 0.3 wavelengths, and scan states exactly on a Wood anomaly. Next to one the solution is benign: the scan impedance follows the expected square-root kink down to 1e-12 of cut-off, finite throughout, and even at the closest floating-point states (|kz| / k about 1e-8) the noise is about 1e-9, consistent with eps / (|kz| / k). NEC's pi() is truncated (3.1415926536), so k and the reciprocal vectors differ by 1.6e-11 and an exact cut-off is reached only by searching for it. Thin-wire modelling error is not part of the estimate. The difference between the thin-wire route of close images and the filament of the smooth kernel is not mainly (a/d)^2, as assumed for issue #24: NEC's reduced kernel evaluates the free-space kernel at sqrt(d^2 + a^2) at every distance, a phase of about k a^2 / (2 d) far from the wire, whereas the field of a tube current differs from a filament only by order (ka)^2 and (a/d)^2. Sending more images through the thin-wire kernel does not converge (the lattice sum of that phase converges only conditionally): from the issue #24 reach to 16 times it, with the thin-wire kernel evaluated by the Gauss rule so that efld_compute's Romberg tolerance plays no part, the scan impedance moves by 7e-6 (1 mm wires, 0.7 x 0.5 wavelength cell, 22 cm end gaps), 1e-5 (10 cm gaps), 3e-5 (5 mm gaps), 6e-5 (stack with 10 mm pitch) and 3e-4 (5 mm wires, 2 cm gaps). That is the order by which a finite NEC array differs from the periodic result for this reason alone.
  • issue #26: perfect ground. The ground images of the other cells, mirrored in z=0 with the opposite current along the mirrored segment (NEC's frati = 1), go through the same correction as the direct images: add_periodic_images runs once for the source and once for its mirror, with close images through efld_compute at zi + zj and -salpj. The close-image list of the direct images serves both, since for conductors above the plane |p - q'| >= |p - q| for every pair of points; conductors that touch, nearly touch or cross the plane are refused by the image-contact rule against their own ground image. Ground connections stay unsupported. Image theory: a dipole over ground equals a free-space cell with the dipole and its mirror driven oppositely, Z = Z00 - Z01, to 8e-14 (horizontal) and 5e-14 (tilted). A tapered 15 x 15 finite array over the same ground (horizontal dipoles at a quarter wavelength, off broadside) matches the centre element to 1.9 %, 0.9 % at 21 x 21; without the ground images of the other cells the periodic result is 86 % off. Reciprocity Z(psi) = Z(-psi)^T holds to 7.8e-5. Literature: Stark (1966) gives the scan resistance of half-wave dipoles a quarter wavelength over ground in closed form, R = R_bs SF (his eq. 20, Wheeler's broadside value times a scanning factor), from a sinusoidal current by induced EMF; without the image term the same expansion gives the free-space array. His figure 8 (a = b = 0.5 wavelengths) puts the dipoles end to end, a connected array, so the test uses 0.6 along and 0.5 across the dipoles (radius 1e-5 wavelengths, 21 segments). Broadside is 3.1 % above Stark over ground and 3.3 % in free space, the moment-method current against the sinusoid (the isolated half-wave dipole is 6 % above its EMF value at this radius, 16 % at 1e-3). The scanning factor agrees to 0.4 % in free space (E, H and D planes to 40 degrees, H 40: 1.9 %) and to 1 to 6 % over ground (6.4 % at H 40). Beyond a Wood anomaly Stark's curves part from the moment method: at 0.6 wavelengths over ground, H-plane 56 degrees, the periodic solver gives 272.9 + j145.1 ohm against about 223 ohm read from his figure 9, while the centre of a tapered finite array over the same ground goes from 249.7 + j156.6 (17 x 17) to 264.0 + j152.0 (25 x 25), towards the periodic value. Stark's crosses come from Carter's mutual impedances, which assume the same sinusoidal current.
  • issue #27: Wood anomalies. Floquet modes with |kz| < 1e-3 k are split out of the spectral sum: the kernel keeps only the remainder (f(kz) - 2) / kz of each (by a Taylor series through kz^3 where |kz| (|dz| + 1/E) < 1e-3), and the singular part exp(-j kt . r) / (2 j A kz) enters the matrix analytically. Its segment field is (-j eta / k) [k^2 s - kt (kt . s)] int I S, so the matrix gains -eta k / (2 A kz) times a TE and a TM rank-one term (e = z x t and z) and -eta kz / (2 A k) times a longitudinal one. The longitudinal term is added to the matrix; the TE and TM terms form a low-rank update solved by the Woodbury identity on the LU of the rest, which needs only C^-1 = -2 A kz / (eta k), zero on cut-off. Over perfect ground the ground image cancels the TE and longitudinal terms and doubles TM. Several modes at cut-off at once couple through linearly dependent columns (a straight dipole sees (0, 1) and (0, -1) alike), which makes the small matrix singular exactly there; a pseudo-inverse that drops singular values below 1e-11 of the largest solves it, the dropped directions being those the columns map to zero. Splitting every mode within |kz| < 1.3 k instead changes the scan impedance by 5e-14 (2e-14 over ground). Exactly on cut-off (searched in floating point) the scan impedance is finite and approaches its neighbours like 24.7 sqrt(delta) from both sides, through the split boundary. Along sweeps of the scan phases on a rectangular and a hexagonal lattice, the kinks of the scan impedance (outstanding local maxima of its second difference) coincide with the Wood circles predicted from the reciprocal lattice, four per sweep. The refusal of a scan state exactly on a Wood anomaly is lifted; results within |kz| < 1e-3 k carry the point status near-cutoff, and the rounding term of the estimated error is at most eps / 1e-3. NEC's pi() and its speed of light (from 8.854e-12) both differ from the exact values, so a Wood circle computed outside the engine is off by about 1e-5 in k unless it uses the engine's constants.
  • issue #28: scan sweeps. The fill (about 12 us per kernel evaluation for the spatial and as much for the spectral sum, at the automatic splitting) was measured at 40 to 250 times a finite solve of the same cell, so caching only the free-space matrix, as first planned, saves about 1 %. The spatial term of each lattice point rho depends on the scan state only through the phase exp(-j kt0 . rho), and the splitting parameter depends only on the lattice and k, so the fill is split into the free-space matrix, one matrix per lattice point (its spatial term, with close images through NEC's thin-wire segment field and ground images, integrated per image) and the spectral sum. The first two are kept per frequency (256 MiB at most); a scan change fills only the spectral sum and adds the phase-weighted image matrices. In WASM, 84 segments: 2.2 s per scan state before, 1.85 s at a new frequency and 0.97 s for a scan change after; 22 segments: 155 ms before, 130 ms and 67 ms after. Scan changes, frequency, load, ground and lattice changes in sequence match a new model to 1e-12. Sweeps run on a pool of worker periodic models that replay the cell's configuration.
  • issue #29: Floquet-mode amplitudes. Per port basis solve, the radiation vector N(k_pq) of the cell current (with the ground image over perfect ground) comes from closed-form segment integrals of 1, sin ks, cos ks against exp(j k_pq . r) with complex kz, which also replace the 16-point Gauss rule of the split-mode moments (issue #27). Amplitudes are TE (phi-hat) and TM (theta-hat) components of E_pq, power-normalized (P = |a|^2 / 2 per cell, peak phasors) and referred to the origin, so the (0,0) amplitude above the cell is the scan element pattern. With the thin-wire eta of the matrix (376.73 ohm), port input power equals the propagating Floquet power plus load loss to 3.5e-3, 9.5e-4, 2.4e-4 and 4.7e-5 at 11, 21, 41 and 81 segments (broadside half-wave dipole): the moment method's own power error, second order in the segment length, the same at oblique scan, with a grating lobe, with loads, over ground and for two-port cells. Against finite arrays of 11 x 11 to 27 x 27 half-wave dipoles (0.6 x 0.5 wavelengths, 30 degrees in the E- and H-planes), the embedded pattern of the centre element alone ripples around the periodic value by 1 to 6 %, the edge waves of the finite array; driven with the scan phases under a Gaussian taper (sigma 1.6 m, 15 x 15), the weighted mean of the inner elements' patterns matches to 0.04 % (E-plane) and 0.37 % (H-plane). Terminated amplitudes (every port in Zg, one driven with 1 W available) derived from the raw amplitudes and the scan impedance match a fresh solve with Zg as port loads to 1e-9.
  • issue #30: scan element pattern object. Near the Wood circle of mode c the (0,0) amplitude is analytic in u, v and kappa_c = |kz_c| / k, but not only a square-root kink: through the low-rank update its solve carries (kappa_c D + S)^-1, and with S small the pattern changes within kappa_c ~ 0.01. A half-wave dipole array in free space (0.7 x 0.6 wavelengths) drops to a null on the circle of its (1, 0) grating lobe (|a| = 0.003 against 0.11 nearby, Z near -j 918 ohm), whose width varies along the circle within 0.1 rad; the unit-current amplitude is smoother but has its own transition within 1e-3. The pattern is therefore sampled on rows graded geometrically from kappa = 1e-3 on both sides of every circle and interpolated in a lift of kappa that spaces them evenly, never across a circle. At the horizon the amplitude goes like sqrt(cos theta) with a finite limit of a / sqrt(cos theta) (to 1e-4 between cos theta = 1e-3 and 1e-4, free space and ground); below 1e-4 NEC's pi, 1.6e-11 above the exact value, shifts the engine's cut-off, so directions near the horizon use the kernel's wavenumber. Adaptive refinement (1 % of the peak) resolves the null: at spacing 0.2 with refinement to 4596 samples the error next to it is at most 1.2e-3 (1 % of the peak), against 20 to 350 % of the local value on the fixed grid of spacing 0.1. Contrary to an argument that a guided wave along a wire array cannot be phase-matched in visible space (its (0,0) harmonic would radiate), x-dipoles 0.25 wavelengths over ground with three layers of shorter parasitic dipoles (half-length 0.22 at 0.4, 0.55 and 0.7 wavelengths, rows 0.65 apart) show a true scan-blindness notch in the H-plane at v = 0.452, 0.086 before the (0, -1) onset, down to 0.1 to 0.4 % of the peak (11 and 5 segments per wire): at blindness the (0,0) harmonic of the guided wave has zero amplitude. The stated interpolation error covered 397 of 399 random directions; the two misses, by up to 3 times, lie where two Wood circles, or one and the horizon, are both close.
  • issue #31: windowed arrays. windowedArray(pattern, { steering, cells, excitation? }) forms the array factor over the given lattice cells, with the excitation times the steering phases of a scan result, and multiplies it by the scan element pattern; it offers the steering state's scan impedance as every driven element's active impedance, flagged approximate. Against direct finite solves of x-dipoles (three segments, 0.6 x 0.5 wavelengths) driven with the same port voltages and steered to 30 degrees in the E-plane, the largest difference in the main beam and near sidelobes (four null widths either side, above and below the array) is 7.8, 4.8 and 2.2 % of the peak at 8 x 8, 16 x 16 and 32 x 32; under a cosine taper at 16 x 16, 0.5 % (H-plane, 30 degrees) and 1.0 % (theta 40, phi 45 degrees). The centre element of the uniform 32 x 32 array sees the offered active impedance to 0.7 %. Vertical dipoles on the same lattice, coupled strongly in every direction of the plane, converge much more slowly (110, 47 and 19 %). Gaussian-tapered finite arrays of them (15, 21 and 29 cells a side) move steadily towards the scan element pattern at the steering direction (E_theta -0.58 + 1.12j, -0.73 + 1.21j, -0.83 + 1.22j against -0.95 + 1.16j), which points to slow edge convergence rather than an error of the pattern; no test covers this case.
  • issue #32: layered radome above the array. setLayeredStacks({ above: { bottomZM, layers } }) with lossy dielectric layers (eps = eps_r (1 - j tan delta)). A transmission-line model per mode and polarization (nec_periodic_stack; admittance kz / k for TE, impedance kz / (eps k) for TM, so nothing is infinite at cut-off) gives the voltage reflection, transmission and absorbed power; it is tested natively against the Fresnel half-space, Brewster's angle, the half-wave window, the thin-sheet susceptance (eps_r - 1) k t of WAIM theory, lossless energy conservation (also for evanescent modes) and reciprocity of the transmission. In the fill each mode adds c R_eff a b^T per polarization, c = -eta k / (2 A kz), with b the modal integrals of the basis functions (with the ground image), a the down-going wave at the observation points (with its ground reflection), phases referred to the stack's lower face h, and R_eff = R / (1 - rho R exp(-2j kz h)) the reflections between stack and perfect ground (rho = -1 TE, +1 TM on theta-hat amplitudes, on which TM reflects as minus the voltage reflection). Modes are kept down to the decay exp(-18) over twice the gap: measured truncation error at most 0.03 exp(-decay) for decays of 6 to 26 (gaps 0.02 to 0.2 wavelengths, free space and ground, eps_r 4 and 10). Above 20,000 modes (a gap of about 0.01 wavelengths in a half- wavelength cell) the stack is refused with the gap and mode count; a wire reaching the stack is refused. Large terms (near cut-off, near a guided-wave pole) go to the low-rank update, with columns now scaled to unit size. Near cut-off the stack's TE and TM terms nearly cancel the kernel's split singular parts (grazing incidence on any dielectric reflects as -1 in TE and +1 in TM), so for a split mode the two are joined into one term c (1 + R_eff sigma / sf) u v^T plus the finite rest c R_eff (a b^T - sigma u v^T); left as two nearly parallel low-rank columns, the pseudo-inverse dropped their difference and the scan impedance fell back to the bare array's value at cut-off (-1053j against -75j). A mode exactly on cut-off is evaluated at |kz| = sqrt(eps) k, the nearest representable state. Floquet modes above the cell are the transmitted waves, referred to the origin; below it (free space) they include the reflection; dielectricLossW sums the absorbed power over all modes, evanescent ones included. Verified at the package seam:
    • a layer of eps_r 1 changes nothing (to 1e-12; it reflects exactly 0);
    • a layer of loss tangent 1e12 acts as a ground plane at its lower face: the scan impedance matches the mirrored cell over perfect ground by the image route to 1e-5, independently checking signs and polarizations;
    • thinning a layer (1e-2, 1e-3, 1e-4 m) leaves a tenth of the change each time;
    • power closes to 6.6e-4 to 9.6e-4 at 21 segments, the moment method's own error without a stack, with 10 to 34 % of the input lost in the stack, over ground and in free space, including a sheet 0.05 wavelengths from the wires that absorbs mostly from evanescent modes;
    • the stack's change of the scan impedance is reciprocal to 1.3e-4;
    • the transmitted wave carries the slab's own phase where the (0,0) mode crosses without reflection: the bare array's amplitudes times T exp(j kz d), with T = -1 through a half-wave window at broadside (TE and TM) and T = exp(-j kz1 d) for TM at the Brewster angle, to 1e-5; the scan element pattern swept with a stack equals the transmitted (0,0) amplitude at its samples;
    • through a Wood anomaly, bisected to floating-point resolution, the scan impedance is continuous (both sides within 1e-7 to 1.3e-6, as without a stack);
    • against CST Studio Suite 2024 (frequency-domain unit cell, Floquet port, second-order tetrahedra; test/fixtures/fem-issue32): 48 x 2 mm strips 25 mm over ground on 60 x 50 mm at 2.998 GHz, a 3 mm sheet of eps_r 4, tan delta 0.02 at 40 mm, eight scan states to 60 degrees. The transmitted (0,0) TE and TM fractions and the sheet loss agree to 3.3e-4 (CST's own power balance closes to 3e-4), and the sheet's change of the impedance to 3.0 to 4.3 % (21 segments, wire radius w / 4). The absolute impedances differ by about 5 ohm in both cases, the feed models (a 1 mm face port against a segment source). The thin-wire result moves with the segmentation once segments get short: at H-plane 60 degrees 218, 243, 258 and 270 ohm resistance at 11, 21, 31 and 41 uniform segments (CST 246). Holding the feed segment at 2.4 mm and refining only the arms does not converge either: the bare broadside resistance goes 107, 127, 138, 145 and 156 ohm for 5 to 30 segments per arm (CST 120), and the sheet's change matches CST best at 10 per arm (4 to 5 %, against 10 to 30 % elsewhere). The same happens in an isolated finite dipole of the same wire in free space, with no periodic code involved (71, 85, 98, 106 and 119 ohm for 5, 10, 20, 30 and 60 segments per arm; with a 0.1 mm wire 69 to 85 ohm, settling slowly). It is NEC's thin-wire kernel below about 3 radii per segment and its source model next to much shorter segments, so the comparison uses uniform segments of 4.6 radii (21); a feed-gap-matched model would need tapered segmentation.

The design decisions taken after the evaluation are collected in Decisions. Where they change an earlier section, that section says so. Terms follow GLOSSARY.md; the layered-stack route is recorded in ADR 0001.

Goal

  1. Define an antenna configuration (the unit cell) and a 2D lattice, and get back the scan impedance and scan admittance (Z and Y) of the unit-cell ports for given scan states.
  2. As an additional feature, a Floquet port at a defined height above the cell: Floquet-mode amplitudes radiated by the cell, and eventually a generalized scattering matrix between circuit ports and Floquet modes.

Both must be usable from the WASM package (packages/necpp-wasm) that the visualizer consumes.

Verdict

Feasible. The periodic Z/Y is a contained change in the C++ matrix fill plus additive ABI and TypeScript surface. The Floquet port is mostly post-processing on current data the package already exports, plus one new excitation type for the receive direction.

The main reason it fits well: the periodic Green's function can be split into the free-space term that NEC already handles (including all the singular self-term machinery) and a smooth remainder that only needs low-order quadrature. No existing kernel code has to be rewritten.

Observations that matter for the design:

  • Inside the matrix fill, lengths are normalized to the wavelength (the kernel is called with xk = 2*pi). The time convention is exp(+jwt), the kernel is exp(-jkR)/R.
  • NEC uses point matching at segment centres, so there is no testing integral; each matrix entry is a field evaluation.
  • Each segment carries three current terms (constant, sin ks, cos ks). The field of each is returned separately (exk/eyk/ezk, exs/.., exc/..) and combined through ax/bx/cx.
  • The stateful model and the WASM ABI expose voltage and current drives only. Plane-wave excitation exists in the legacy card path but is not reachable from the stateful model.
  • The repository had no complex error function (erfc of complex argument); the periodic kernel unit now provides one.
  • The LU solver is general (not symmetric-only), so a non-symmetric interaction matrix is acceptable to the solver itself.

Formulation

Lattice and scan

Lattice vectors a1, a2 in the xy-plane, cell area A = |a1 x a2|, reciprocal vectors b1, b2 with a_i . b_j = 2*pi*delta_ij. The current in cell (m, n) equals the unit-cell current times exp(-j k_t0 . rho_mn) with rho_mn = m a1 + n a2.

The scan state is the transverse wavevector k_t0. Two equivalent inputs:

  • scan direction: k_t0 = k sin(theta) (cos(phi), sin(phi));
  • inter-element phases: psi_i = -k_t0 . a_i.

Phases are the better primary input: they also cover invisible space (|k_t0| > k), which is needed if the scan admittance is later integrated over the Brillouin zone. (theta, phi) is a convenience on top. The phases are always relative to the user's a1, a2; any internal basis reduction is mapped back before results are returned.

Lattice types

All five planar lattice types are special cases of the general a1, a2 above, so the kernel, the Floquet-mode indexing and the Bloch phase need no per-type code. Named presets exist only in TypeScript.

Lattice a1, a2 Array-literature name
Square (d,0), (0,d) square grid
Rectangular (dx,0), (0,dy) rectangular grid
Centered rectangular (dx,0), (dx/2,dy) staggered / offset-row grid
Hexagonal (d,0), (d/2, d*sqrt(3)/2) equilateral triangular grid
Oblique anything else skewed grid

Three implementation details depend on the lattice:

  • Spatial and spectral sums are truncated by radius, not by an index box |m|,|n| <= M, which converges poorly for skewed lattices.
  • A strongly skewed input basis is reduced internally (Lagrange/Gauss reduction) to keep the term count low.
  • Clearance is checked as the distance from each conductor to the periodic images of the unit cell's conductors (its own and the others'), not to a cell wall, which has no unique shape for a non-rectangular lattice.

Honeycomb, kagome and dual-polarized arrays with offset sub-lattices are not lattices; they are a lattice with several elements per unit cell. The hexagonal lattice's six nearest Wood-anomaly circles sit further from the visible region than the square lattice's four for the same element density, which is the classic reason it allows about 13 % fewer elements for the same grating-lobe-free scan range.

Periodic Green's function

Spatial form (conditionally convergent, not usable directly): 

G_p(r, r') = sum_mn exp(-j k_t0 . rho_mn) exp(-j k R_mn) / (4 pi R_mn)
R_mn = | r - r' - rho_mn |

Spectral (Floquet) form, with k_tpq = k_t0 + p b1 + q b2 and kz_pq = sqrt(k^2 - |k_tpq|^2) on the branch with Im(kz) <= 0: 

G_p = 1/(2 j A) * sum_pq exp(-j k_tpq . (rho - rho')) exp(-j kz_pq |z - z'|) / kz_pq

The spectral form converges exponentially only when |z - z'| > 0, so it is useless for a planar or thin cell but is exactly what the Floquet port needs.

Ewald form, usable everywhere, with splitting parameter E (signs and prefactors verified in the [periodic_green] suite): 

G_p = G_spat + G_spec

G_spat = 1/(8 pi) * sum_mn exp(-j k_t0 . rho_mn) / R_mn *
         [ exp(-j k R_mn) erfc(R_mn E - j k/(2E))
         + exp(+j k R_mn) erfc(R_mn E + j k/(2E)) ]

G_spec = 1/(4 j A) * sum_pq exp(-j k_tpq . (rho - rho')) / kz_pq *
         [ exp(-j kz_pq dz) erfc(j kz_pq/(2E) - dz E)
         + exp(+j kz_pq dz) erfc(j kz_pq/(2E) + dz E) ],   dz = z - z'

Both sums converge like a Gaussian. For cells larger than about one wavelength the optimal E = sqrt(pi / A) causes loss of significance (terms of size exp((k/2E)^2) cancelling); E must then be raised so that k/(2E) stays bounded, at the cost of more spectral terms.

As implemented: E = max(sqrt(pi / A), |Re(k)| / 4), so k/(2E) <= 2. Both sums keep every term whose Gaussian factor exceeds exp(-36): lattice points within a radius of the observation point (spatial), and Floquet modes with |k_tpq| <= sqrt(|k|^2 + 144 E^2) (spectral), enumerated over Gauss-reduced lattice vectors. A half-wavelength cell then needs about 40 terms per sum rather than 9 to 25; the fixed tolerance (issue #25) keeps the kernel at 1e-10, well inside it. The complex error function uses Weideman's rational approximation of the Faddeeva function (40 terms, about 1e-13 relative), with exp(a) erfc(z) combined so neither factor overflows.

Splitting off what NEC already does

Write 

G_p = G_free(0,0) + G_smooth
G_smooth = G_spec + G_spat(without its (0,0) term) + [ G_spat(0,0) - G_free ]

The bracket is finite at R -> 0 (the 1/R singularities cancel), so G_smooth is smooth everywhere inside the cell. With f(R) = exp(jkR) erfc(RE + jk/(2E)) the bracket equals (f(R) - f(-R)) / (8 pi R); near R = 0 the implementation uses its Taylor series in R^2 (odd derivatives of f through Hermite polynomials) instead of the difference.

  • G_free(0,0) is the existing NEC interaction, unchanged, including the self term, the extended thin-wire kernel and the far-segment lumped approximation.
  • G_smooth is the only new physics. Because it is smooth, the field it produces can be computed with a low-order Gauss rule per source segment (as implemented: 6 points, see below).

If conductors come close to a cell wall, the nearest images make G_smooth nearly singular. The remedy is to move the nearest images out of G_smooth and evaluate them through the existing efld_compute with a displaced source and a phase factor, the same way the ground image is handled today. Those image fields are scan-independent apart from the phase factor. As implemented (issue #24), the images moved out are chosen by distance per observation point and source segment rather than as a fixed 3x3 ring; in practice the thin-wire kernel, not the Gauss rule, is what the close images need.

Field of a segment under the smooth kernel

For a current I(s') along unit vector s' on one segment, with G including the 1/(4 pi): 

E(r) = (-j eta / k) * [ k^2 s' * int I G ds'
                       + int (dI/ds') grad G ds'
                       - ( I grad G ) evaluated between the segment ends ]

grad is with respect to the observation point. Only G_smooth and its gradient are needed, never second derivatives. The end terms do not cancel per segment (NEC's basis has non-zero current at segment ends), so they must be kept; they cancel only in the sum over connected segments.

This is evaluated three times per source segment, for I = 1, sin(k s'), cos(k s'), and added to exk..ezc before cmww projects on the observation segment. Unlike the free-space term, the result is a full 3-vector; the local (rho, z) decomposition used in efld_compute does not apply because the lattice sum is not axially symmetric about the source segment.

Verified (issue #21): fed with the plain free-space kernel, the routine matches efld_compute (exact thin-wire branch) to 5.5e-11 for the sine and cosine terms and the transverse part of the constant term in parallel, collinear, skewed, near and far geometries. The axial part of the constant term agrees to about 1e-5, the accuracy of NEC's own Romberg integral (intx, tolerance 1e-4). Two details came out of that comparison:

  • eta must be 376.73 ohm, the value fixed in eksc/ekscx (j 4.771341189 = j eta / (8 pi^2)), not em::impedance() (about 376.7343). The two differ by 1.1e-5, and the smooth correction is added to the eksc fill.
  • Under the smooth kernel the Gauss rule converges spectrally. Six points keep the error near 2e-11 for segments up to a tenth of a wavelength. On short segments the charge and end-charge terms cancel to about (kL)^2, which amplifies kernel rounding. To keep that rounding near 1e-11, the origin term of G_smooth uses its Taylor series through R^14 up to R (E + |k|) = 0.3.

Ground

  • Perfect ground: the image source gets the same treatment as the direct source (free-space image term from existing code, plus a smooth correction for the image lattice).
  • Finite ground (soil of infinite extent, GroundModel kind: "finite"): not in stage 2. It arrives in stage 3b as one case of a layered stack: in the spectral domain each Floquet mode sees an exact Fresnel coefficient, so no Sommerfeld integrals are needed.

Layered stacks (stage 3b)

Decided in ADR 0001; the stack above the wires is implemented as issue #32 (see the status entry). The wires live in an air region. Above and below it, layered stacks that are uniform in the lattice plane (radome, substrate below the wires, finite ground) act on each Floquet mode (p, q) only through a TE and a TM reflection coefficient from a transmission-line model of the stack. Such stacks do not couple Floquet modes.

Each mode leaves a source segment, reflects off the stack and returns to an observation segment, which adds 

Z_refl = sum_pq U_pq Gamma_pq V_pq^T

a sum of rank-2 (TE + TM) terms. V_pq is the modal segment integral the Floquet port already computes in stage 3, so the code is shared. The terms decay like exp(-|kz_pq| * 2 g) over the air gap g, so they need no Ewald treatment. Perfect ground stays on the image route, because reflection terms converge badly for wires close to the ground.

  • A conductor that enters a stack is rejected.
  • The mode count is chosen from the decay against the fixed tolerance; above a mode cap the configuration is refused, never silently truncated. The gap and mode count are reported in the diagnostics.
  • With a stack above the array, the embedded (0,0) amplitude is the wave transmitted through the stack.

Engine changes for periodic Z/Y

  1. Periodic kernel unit (new file, e.g. nec_periodic_green.{h,cpp}): lattice setup, Ewald evaluation of G_smooth and its gradient, complex erfc (Faddeeva function; needs a small self-contained implementation with a compatible licence), Wood-anomaly detection.
  2. Context state: lattice vectors, scan phases, a periodic-mode flag on nec_context. In periodic mode force nop = 1 (no symmetry reduction).
  3. Matrix fill: in cmww (or a sibling called from cmset), add the smooth-kernel field to the nine field components before projection.
  4. Caching: the free-space matrix depends on frequency only. Keep a copy so that a scan change needs only the correction fill and a new LU. Memory is one extra N x N complex matrix.
  5. Stateful model: set_lattice, set_scan; invalidate the factorization on scan change; reject unsupported configurations (below).
  6. C ABI (additive): e.g. necpp_wasm_v1_set_lattice, necpp_wasm_v1_set_scan_phases, necpp_wasm_v1_clear_lattice; add to the exported-function list in src/CMakeLists.txt.
  7. TypeScript: a separate PeriodicModel type (decided), worker protocol entries, lattice presets, and a scan sweep over caller-given scan states that runs on the worker pool, can be cancelled and delivers results in chunks.
  8. Wood anomalies: Floquet modes near cut-off are split out of the spectral sum. Their singular part tends to (2/gamma) times a separable rank-2 (TE + TM) term, independent of dz; solving with a low-rank (Woodbury) update on the LU keeps port quantities finite and smooth in gamma, including gamma = 0 exactly. The per-point status marks "on or near cut-off"; NaN is reserved for genuine failure.
  9. Tolerance: one fixed internal tolerance for Ewald sums, mode truncation and stack modes. No truncation parameters in the API; each result carries an estimated error. A configuration that cannot reach the tolerance is refused.

Everything downstream of the matrix (loads, port definition, Y by unit-voltage solves, Z by inversion, power budget) works unchanged. The result at each scan point is the P x P scan admittance of the P unit-cell ports.

Restrictions for a first version

  • 2D lattice in the xy-plane only, parallel to any ground. A 1D (linear) periodic array needs a different Green's function and would get its own entry points; 3D stacking is out of scope.
  • Wires only; no surface patches.
  • No conductor may touch its own periodic image, checked by image distance (see connected arrays).
  • Free space or perfect ground only, with a layered stack above the wires (issue #32); stacks below the wires and finite ground follow in issue #33.
  • Geometry symmetry reduction disabled.
  • Networks and transmission lines: untested in this mode; exclude until checked.

Things that change meaning in periodic mode

  • Reciprocity: Z(psi) is not symmetric. The relation is Z(psi) = Z(-psi)^T. Any code that assumes or enforces Z = Z^T must be reviewed, in the engine and in the TS layer.
  • Far field: radiation patterns of a single cell are meaningless. They are replaced by Floquet-mode amplitudes. The existing far-field, embedded-field and ring-field calls should be rejected in periodic mode.
  • Power: the budget is per unit cell.

Cost estimate

Per scan point, for N segments and Q quadrature points: N^2 (Q + 2) kernel evaluations, each roughly 20 to 50 terms with one or two complex erfc. For N = 100 that is a few million erfc calls, which I would expect to land around 0.1 s in WASM, plus one LU of an N x N matrix. A dense 2D scan sweep (thousands of points) is therefore tens of seconds to minutes on one worker and would benefit from the existing worker pool or from interpolating G_smooth on a difference grid. None of this has been measured.

Floquet port

Transmit: circuit port to Floquet modes

Above all conductors, the field of the periodic current is a sum of Floquet modes E_pq exp(-j k_pq . r) with k_pq = (k_tpq, kz_pq): 

N(k) = integral over the unit-cell current of J(r') exp(+j k . r')
E_pq = -( eta / (2 A k kz_pq) ) * [ k^2 N(k_pq) - k_pq ( k_pq . N(k_pq) ) ]

and the amplitude at the port plane z = h carries the extra factor exp(-j kz_pq h). Below the cell the same holds with -kz_pq. With a perfect ground, add the image current to N.

  • For a propagating mode, N(k_pq) is the same segment integral the far-field code evaluates at the direction of k_pq. So the amplitude is the existing far-field quantity scaled by 1 / (A cos(theta_pq)).
  • For an evanescent mode, kz_pq is imaginary. The closed-form segment integral of (A + B sin ks + C cos ks) exp(j k . r') is the same expression with a complex direction cosine; this needs a small generalization.
  • The inputs (segment centres, tangents, lengths, A/B/C coefficients) are already exported by getCurrentDistribution. Decided: the amplitudes are nevertheless computed in C++, because the stage 3b matrix fill needs the same modal segment integrals and sweeps run to thousands of scan points.
  • Amplitudes per port are normalized as EmbeddedFieldNormalization: unit voltage with the other ports shorted, or unit current with them open. Generator-referred, terminated versions are derived in TypeScript from the scan impedance.

Modes are split into TE and TM with respect to z and power-normalized. The power carried per cell by a propagating mode is P_pq = A |E_pq|^2 kz_pq / (2 eta k) (peak phasors).

By-products:

  • Scan element pattern: the (0,0) amplitude as a function of scan is the element pattern in the infinite array.
  • Power check: port input power equals the sum of propagating Floquet power (above and below) plus ohmic loss. This is a strong built-in test.

Receive and Floquet-to-Floquet

This needs an incident Floquet mode as excitation: a plane wave whose transverse wavevector matches the scan state, so the Bloch condition is met automatically. The right-hand side is the negative tangential incident field at each match point (plus the ground reflection when a ground is present), modelled on etmns_linear_wave. It must be added to the stateful model and the ABI as a new drive type.

With that:

  • port currents under termination give the Floquet-to-port entries (these can be cross-checked against the transmit entries at the reversed scan through reciprocity);
  • scattered Floquet amplitudes, plus the direct term of the incident wave, give the Floquet-to-Floquet block. This also makes the engine usable for frequency-selective-surface style problems.

Without a ground plane a second port below the cell is required for the transmitted wave. Only propagating modes need to be supported as incident modes; evanescent modes are useful as outputs when h is small.

Finite-array pattern from the periodic result

A windowed array (a finite block of driven elements inside the infinite array, all other elements present and terminated) has the pattern 

E(theta, phi) = g_inf(theta, phi) * sum_mn a_mn exp(+j k r_hat . rho_mn)
a_mn = w_mn exp(-j k_t0 . rho_mn)

exactly, where g_inf(theta, phi) is the scan element pattern: the (0,0) Floquet amplitude at the scan state k_t = k sin(theta) (cos(phi), sin(phi)). It therefore needs a scan sweep over the visible region, not only the steering state. Only truncation (edge effects) is left out; a direct finite solve with array-solver.ts is the exact reference.

  • Approximation, flagged: every driven element is taken to see the scan impedance of the steering direction. Per-element active impedances of the windowed array need the Brillouin-zone mutual coupling below and come later. Until then, the finite-array solve gives exact per-element active impedances for arrays of moderate size without a radome.
  • Sampling: g_inf comes from a sweep on an auto u-v grid inside the unit circle (spacing from the unit-cell size, with a manual override), refined along both sides of the Wood-anomaly circles. These are known in advance: unit circles centred on the reciprocal-lattice points scaled by 1/k. Interpolation never crosses a circle.
  • API: the package exposes the result as a scan element pattern object that can be evaluated at any (u, v), so any consumer field grid and display frame works. windowedArray(pattern, { steering, cells, excitation? }) forms the array factor from a scan result at the steering state (scan phases and scan impedance) and the cells' lattice indices, and returns the far field in the finite models' FarFieldResult form.
  • Termination consistency: the windowed model is exactly periodic only if the passive elements' termination equals the generators' internal impedance (short with ideal voltage drive, open with ideal current drive, or a common generator impedance).
  • What stays honest: the infinite model overstates scan blindness. A radome guided wave needs many cells to build up, so a small real array shows a shallower, wider and shifted dip. The notch also appears in every steered pattern, because g_inf multiplies them all.

Mutual coupling from the periodic result

The coupling between the reference element and element (m, n) in the infinite array is the Fourier coefficient of the scan admittance over the Brillouin zone: 

Y_mn = 1/(4 pi^2) * double integral over psi1, psi2 of
       Y(psi1, psi2) exp(-j (m psi1 + n psi2))      [verify sign convention]

The integrand stays finite on the Wood-anomaly curves but has square-root kinks there, so a uniform grid converges slowly in the far couplings and the quadrature needs care. A uniform N x N grid followed by a 2D FFT gives the couplings of an array that repeats every N cells (aliased), which is a usable first approximation. This is optional and purely a TS-side feature.

Why deferred (stage 5): the existing finite-array solve already answers "what does each element of my array see?" for arrays of moderate size, and it does better: real edges, no aliasing, scan blindness damped correctly. The Brillouin route gives a windowed array (elements outside the window present and shorted), not a finite one, and costs a full scan-phase sweep including invisible space. It only becomes worthwhile where the finite solve cannot go:

  • Layered stacks (after stage 3b): NEC-2 has no dielectric layers, so this is the only way to get element-to-element coupling and per-element active impedance with a radome or substrate included.
  • Very large arrays, where the finite MoM becomes too slow. One sweep then serves every window size, shape and taper.
  • Isolating edge effects, by comparing the finite array against the windowed array of the same size.

Connected arrays

Elements that connect to their own periodic image (tightly coupled dipoles, wire grids, long-slot style structures) need junction basis functions that span the cell boundary with a Bloch phase on the neighbour segment's coefficients. This touches NEC's connection logic (icon1/icon2, trio, tbf/sbf in c_geometry.cpp), not only the kernel. It is tractable but a separate piece of work, and analytic wire-grid references only become usable for validation once it exists.

Validation plan

  • Kernel: Ewald result against a direct lattice sum with slight loss, and against the spectral series for |dz| > 0; independence of the result from the splitting parameter.
  • Field routine: smooth-kernel routine fed with the free-space kernel against efld_compute.
  • Limits: very large lattice spacing should approach the isolated-element impedance.
  • Finite array: centre-element active impedance of a large uniformly phased finite array from array-solver.ts should approach the periodic result (slowly, with edge ripple).
  • Literature: published scan-impedance curves for infinite planar dipole arrays with and without a ground plane.
  • Power: port power against Floquet power plus loss for every test case.
  • Reciprocity: Z(psi) = Z(-psi)^T; transmit and receive Floquet entries.
  • Lattice types: centre-element active impedance of large finite hexagonal and centered-rectangular arrays from array-solver.ts against the periodic scan impedance.
  • Windowed pattern: against direct finite solves at 8x8, 16x16 and 32x32, showing the edge error shrinking.
  • Wood anomalies: circle positions predicted from the reciprocal lattice against the observed kinks in the scan impedance; finite values exactly on a circle.
  • Layered stacks: an eps_r = 1 layer changes nothing; thin-layer limit; power closure including dielectric loss; a wide-angle impedance matching (WAIM) sheet case. Magill and Wheeler (IEEE Trans. AP-14(1):49-53, 1966) turned out to model aperture windows flush with a metal face, not dipoles, and no dipole case with every dimension given could be verified, so the reference is a full-wave FEM unit-cell simulation run for issue #32 (see the status entry).
  • Finite ground: against NEC's existing reflection-coefficient ground in the large-spacing limit.

Decisions

Taken 2026-11-18. Terms as in GLOSSARY.md.

Scope

  1. The first target is phased-array scan impedance. Frequency-selective surfaces follow with the Floquet excitation in stage 4. Work is in the necpp backend only (engine, ABI, necpp-wasm).

Lattice

  1. One general lattice given by a1, a2 in the engine and ABI; named presets only in TypeScript. No latticeType enum.
  2. Honeycomb and sub-lattice arrangements are a lattice with several elements per unit cell, not a lattice type.
  3. 2D periodicity in the xy-plane only, parallel to any ground. No 1D or 3D periodicity, and no ABI placeholders for them.

Scan and API

  1. Scan phases (psi1, psi2) along the user's a1, a2 are the primary input; (theta, phi) is a convenience. Internal basis reduction is mapped back.
  2. A separate PeriodicModel type in TypeScript, without far-field calls.
  3. The P x P result is the scan impedance / scan admittance; active impedance keeps its per-drive meaning.
  4. Floquet amplitudes are computed in C++, normalized as EmbeddedFieldNormalization; terminated versions are derived in TypeScript.
  5. Wood anomalies return finite values through a low-rank split of near-cut-off modes; the status flags them and NaN means genuine failure.
  6. One fixed internal tolerance, no truncation parameters, an estimated error in the diagnostics, and refusal when the tolerance cannot be met.

Physics scope

  1. No connected arrays in stage 2; clearance is checked by image distance.
  2. Stage 2 supports free space and perfect ground (image route) only.
  3. Layered stacks (radome, substrate, finite ground) enter through per-mode TE/TM reflection terms in the matrix fill, in a new stage 3b (ADR 0001). Conductors may not enter a stack; too small a gap is refused.

Finite-array pattern

  1. Version 1 is the windowed array: array factor times scan element pattern, with the steering-direction scan impedance for every element flagged as an approximation. Per-element windowed active impedance follows the Brillouin-zone work. The package forms it with a free function over a computed pattern and the steering state's scan result (issue #31), so one sweep serves every window, taper and steering direction.
  2. The scan element pattern comes from an auto u-v sweep (with manual override) refined along the Wood circles, exposed as an object evaluable at any (u, v).
  3. Sweeps run on the worker pool, can be cancelled and deliver results in chunks.
