2// Periodic Green's function kernel for infinite 2D arrays. The matrix fill
3// (nec_context::cmww) adds the field of G_smooth in periodic mode.
4//
5// Conventions (see docs/periodic-floquet-evaluation.md): time dependence
6// exp(+jwt), free-space kernel exp(-jkR)/(4 pi R). The current in cell
7// (m, n) equals the unit-cell current times exp(-j kt0 . rho_mn), with
8// rho_mn = m a1 + n a2, so
9//
10// G_p(r) = sum_mn exp(-j kt0 . rho_mn) exp(-jk R_mn) / (4 pi R_mn),
11// R_mn = | r - rho_mn |,
12//
13// where r is observation minus source. The lattice lies in a plane parallel
14// to xy. Lengths and wavenumbers may use any consistent unit.

102// Periodic Green's function of one lattice, wavenumber and scan state,
103// evaluated by Ewald splitting. Both sums are truncated by radius: the
104// spatial sum over lattice points near the observation point, the spectral
105// sum over Floquet modes with transverse wavevector |kt| below a bound.
106//
107// At a Wood anomaly (kz = 0 for some mode) the spectral sum is singular: the
108// term of a mode tends to its singular part exp(-j kt . r) / (2 j A kz), A
109// the cell area. The modes within near_cutoff_ratio |k| of cut-off are split
110// out: smooth() and full() leave out their singular parts, which stay finite
111// through and exactly on cut-off, and the caller adds the singular parts in a
112// form that cancels 1 / kz (the matrix solve's low-rank update).
